Hey there,

Thinking about hiring a marketing agency but not sure how to sort the mediocre from the magnificent? It’s a big decision - after all, these folks could shape your brand’s voice, strategy, and bottom line. Relying on glossy portfolios and fancy pitches might not be enough to separate the talkers from the doers.

If you really want to vet an agency, you need to dig deeper with the right questions. Below are seven questions that, in my experience, can reveal whether an agency is worth your time (and money).

### 1. “What’s Your Approach to Defining and Measuring Success?”

- **Why It Matters:** If they can’t spell out clear KPIs and tie them back to your core business goals, that’s a massive red flag.
- **What to Listen For:** Terms like ROI, conversion rates, lead quality, or brand awareness—tied to your industry metrics. Vague statements like “We’ll get you more exposure” without specific metrics are a sign they might be winging it.

**Pro Tip:** Ask for a past client story where they improved a measurable metric (e.g., lead-to-sale conversion up by 30%).

### 2. “Can You Walk Me Through a Recent Campaign from Start to Finish?”

- **Why It Matters:** A real campaign breakdown reveals if they have a repeatable process or if they’re just ad-libbing.
- **What to Listen For:** Detailed steps—discovery, strategy, execution, optimization, and analysis. Pay attention to any mention of split-testing, adjusting mid-campaign, or how they handled unexpected roadblocks.

**Pro Tip:** Watch out for oversimplified stories. The best agencies will admit a few hiccups and how they overcame them.

### 3. “Who Handles My Account, and What’s Their Expertise?”

- **Why It Matters:** It’s easy for agencies to trot out their “A-team” during sales pitches, only to delegate your account to junior staff.
- **What to Listen For:** Names, experience levels, and their specific roles. Ask about your point of contact, how often you’ll communicate, and how many accounts they manage simultaneously.

**Pro Tip:** Direct lines of communication are crucial. If you only get a generic support email, that’s a possible red flag for subpar client service.

### 4. “How Do You Stay Updated with Industry Trends and Algorithm Changes?”

- **Why It Matters:** Digital marketing evolves constantly—Google updates, social media algorithm tweaks, new ad formats. An agency that isn’t consistently learning will fall behind, and so will your campaigns.
- **What to Listen For:** Mention of resources like [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/), or official updates from Meta and Google. If they act like they know it all already, that’s a problem.

**Pro Tip:** Ask if they offer any training or workshops for clients. That’s a sign they’re confident in their knowledge and actively share it.

### 5. “What’s Your Communication and Reporting Schedule?”

- **Why It Matters:** A brilliant agency is useless if they go radio silent for weeks. According to Sprout Social, consistent communication increases client satisfaction significantly.
- **What to Listen For:** A regular call or email cadence (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly), transparent dashboards, real-time reporting tools, or at least a quick-turnaround policy for urgent issues.

**Pro Tip:** Ask them to show you a sample report—if it’s all vanity metrics with no context, that’s a clue they might hide behind big, meaningless numbers.

### 6. “How Do You Handle Budget Allocation and Scalability?”

- **Why It Matters:** If your campaign’s initial budget performs well, you might want to scale. The agency needs a plan for managing growth (or even scaling down if results don’t meet expectations).
- **What to Listen For:** Strategies for testing smaller budgets, measuring ROI quickly, and pivoting resources to top-performing channels. A good agency knows how to start lean and then ramp up efficiently.

**Pro Tip:** Check if they’ve done successful scaling for similar clients—maybe they grew a small e-commerce store or boosted a startup’s user base by a factor of 10.

### 7. “Could You Provide References or Case Studies Relevant to My Industry?”

- **Why It Matters:** Every industry has its quirks. Results in one sector don’t always translate to another. If they haven’t worked in your domain, ask how they plan to tackle your niche’s specific challenges.
- **What to Listen For:** Actual numbers, timelines, and an explanation of what worked, what didn’t, and how they overcame hurdles. Flimsy, non-specific anecdotes aren’t enough—demand data.

**Pro Tip:** Call or email at least one reference. The real intel often comes from speaking directly to a past or current client.

### Final Thoughts

Hiring a marketing agency is a big investment. If they dodge these questions or give vague, surface-level answers, you might end up wasting valuable time and money. A good agency will welcome your curiosity, respect your concerns, and have concrete examples to prove they know their stuff.

**What’s Your Next Step?** Take these questions into your next call or meeting. If the agency can answer them confidently and back it up with real data or stories, that’s a promising sign. If they dance around the details, consider it a red flag and keep looking.
